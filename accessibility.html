The standards

Meeting the code isn't the same as finding your way

A building can pass every accessibility check and still leave people lost. The ADA sets minimums for what is built, not for how anyone finds it.

Source: U.S. Department of Justice, 2010 ADA Standards for Accessible Design.

The code covers

  • Ramps that aren't too steep
  • Doors wide enough for a wheelchair
  • Lifts between floors
  • Accessible restrooms

It leaves you to work out

  • Which entrance works for you
  • Which lift reaches the bridge
  • How one building connects to the next
  • What's out of service today

The right door, first

A section view at every level change

At each lift, ramp or stair, a section drawing shows which one to take and which floor to get out on.

Routes for how you move

Step-free, stroller, luggage, fewest level changes, fastest. Choose several and get one route that works for all of them.

Directions start on the sidewalk and carry on through the door and across buildings, all the way to the room.

Directions you can trust

Live elevator status, closures and barrier reports keep your route right, so you don't have to stop and ask.
