From floor plans to routes, automatically.

Buildings already have drawings. WalkAbout reads them and turns every corridor, door, ramp, stair and elevator into a map you can route on and a 3D model you can see.

The technology How it works

How a building becomes navigable

Six stages, automated where they can be and checked by people where it matters. One run per building turns plans into a published map.

  1. Read the plans

    We start from the drawings venues already have: DWG files first, PDFs next. Each floor is scaled to metres and lined up with the floors above and below. Walls, doors, stairs, elevators, ramps and room labels are sorted using the drawing's own layers and labels, with anything unclear flagged for a person to check.

  2. Find every room

    Walls become solid boundaries and every doorway gets a threshold, so each room and corridor becomes its own shape with its own name. Spaces without a label, usually corridors and lobbies, are flagged for review.

  3. Connect the network

    Rooms link through their doors, and corridors along their centrelines. Elevators, stairs and ramps link one floor to the next. Each building is placed on the campus map, so bridges, tunnels and entrances join it to its neighbours and the sidewalks outside.

  4. Weigh every route

    Every link carries what matters for access: width, slope, steps, door type and whether it's open. Your profile changes the weights. Step-free drops any step or slope steeper than 1:12, and fewest level changes avoids floor changes. An elevator outage simply switches its links off.

  5. Build the model and the section

    Automatic checks catch missed doors and dead ends, and our architects review the result. The same run builds a light 3D model that shares coordinates with the map. Your route is drawn through it, and a section is cut along it to show each level change.

  6. Find you inside

    Your phone's barometer and motion sensors are matched to the known floor heights and the map to place you on the right floor. Simple markers at entrances and elevators give a fixed point to correct any drift, with no beacons to install.

Under the hood

For technical readers: what runs where, and what comes out.

Inputs
DWG first, vector PDF next. One file per floor, in metres, floors aligned.
Recognition
Layer, block and label rules for each building, with geometric fallbacks such as door swings and stair treads.
Pipeline
Python, run as one cloud job per upload. Rhino is used for review and fixes, not production.
Outputs
A routing graph (GeoJSON) and a 3D model (glTF) that share coordinates and IDs.
Routing
Weighted shortest paths. Profiles filter or re-weight links when you ask for a route.
Positioning
Barometer, motion sensors and step tracking matched to the map, with markers for a fixed point.
Updates
Status changes switch links off. Manual fixes are kept separately and survive every rerun.
Next
Campus-scale views with outdoor context, then AR directions anchored to the same building map.
